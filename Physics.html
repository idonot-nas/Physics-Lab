📓 Quick Notes & Solutions

1. Density & Buoyancy Simulator

Mass ($m$) 5.0 kg
Volume ($V$) 4.0 L
Density ($\rho$) 1.25 kg/L
$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}, \quad m = \rho \cdot V, \quad F_A = \rho_{liquid} \cdot g \cdot V_{sub}$$
  • $$\rho$$: Body density ($\text{kg/m}^3$ or $\text{kg/L}$)
  • $$F_A$$: Archimedes buoyant force ($N$)
  • Floating condition: If $\rho \le 1.0 \, \text{kg/L}$ — the object floats in water.

3. Pulleys & Simple Machines Simulator

Weight ($P = mg$) 98.1 N
Required Force ($F$) 49.0 N
Force Advantage 2x
$$F = \frac{m \cdot g}{2}, \quad W = F \cdot s, \quad \eta = \frac{W_{useful}}{W_{total}} \cdot 100\%$$
  • Fixed Pulley: Provides no force advantage, only changes the force direction.
  • Movable Pulley: Provides a 2x force advantage ($F = \frac{P}{2}$), but requires twice as much rope distance.

5. Geometric Optics & Refraction Simulator

Angle of Incidence ($\alpha$) 45°
Angle of Refraction ($\beta$) 28.1°
$$n_1 \cdot \sin\alpha = n_2 \cdot \sin\beta, \quad \frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f_0}$$
  • Snell's Law: The ratio of the sines of the angles of incidence and refraction equals the ratio of the refractive indices.
  • Thin Lens Formula: Relates focal length $F$ with object distance $d$ and image distance $f_0$.

📖 Expanded Physics Formula Reference

1. Kinematics & Density

$$\rho = \frac{m}{V}, \quad m = \rho \cdot V, \quad V = \frac{m}{\rho}$$
$$v = \frac{s}{t}, \quad s = v \cdot t, \quad a = \frac{v - v_0}{t}$$

2. Mechanics & Pulleys

$$F_{movable} = \frac{F_{gravity}}{2}, \quad M = F \cdot d, \quad \eta = \frac{W_{useful}}{W_{total}}$$
$$F_{elastic} = k \cdot \Delta x, \quad E_k = \frac{m \cdot v^2}{2}, \quad E_p = m \cdot g \cdot h$$

3. Waves & Geometric Optics

$$v = \lambda \cdot f, \quad T = \frac{1}{f}, \quad n = \frac{c}{v}$$
$$n_1 \sin\alpha = n_2 \sin\beta, \quad D = \frac{1}{F} = \frac{1}{d} + \frac{1}{f_0}$$

4. Electricity & Ohm's Law

$$I = \frac{U}{R}, \quad R = \rho_{conductor} \cdot \frac{l}{S}$$
$$P = U \cdot I = I^2 \cdot R = \frac{U^2}{R}, \quad Q = I^2 \cdot R \cdot t$$
